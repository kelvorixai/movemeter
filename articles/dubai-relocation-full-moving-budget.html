The real cost of relocating to Dubai: a full moving budget

The flight and the first month's rent are the visible costs. The move that actually breaks a budget is the dozen smaller line items that show up in the first six weeks.

Relocating to Dubai involves three cost phases that rarely get budgeted together: the move itself (flights, shipping, temporary housing), the housing setup (deposits, agency fees, utilities connection), and the personal admin (visa, Emirates ID, driving license if applicable). Each phase has its own typical range, and stacking all three gives a far more realistic number than any single "cost of moving to Dubai" headline figure.

Phase 1: the move itself

One-way flights, excess baggage, and any temporary accommodation (hotel or short-term let) while house-hunting typically run the largest single variable in this phase — a family of four with excess baggage and 2-3 weeks of temporary housing can easily run AED 15,000-30,000+ before a single UAE bill arrives. Shipping household goods separately (covered in a companion guide) adds its own cost line if more than suitcases are moving.

Phase 2: housing setup

UAE rent is typically paid in 1-4 cheques upfront rather than monthly, so the "first month's rent" framing understates the real cash outlay. Add a security deposit (commonly 5% of annual rent), a real estate agency commission (commonly 5% of annual rent), Ejari tenancy registration, and DEWA (utilities) connection with its own security deposit, and the setup cost alone commonly runs an additional AED 8,000-15,000 on top of the rent cheques themselves for a mid-range apartment.

Phase 3: personal admin

Employment visa costs are typically covered by the sponsoring employer, but dependent visas, Emirates ID for each family member, medical testing, and (if applicable) converting a foreign driving license all carry separate fees, commonly totaling AED 3,000-8,000 depending on family size — see our dedicated visa cost guides for the detailed breakdown of that stack.

Before the first paycheck lands, a single relocating professional commonly needs somewhere in the AED 20,000-35,000 range in available cash, with families and unfurnished apartments pushing meaningfully higher. This is the number that headline "cost of living" articles tend to leave out entirely.

The actual question before moving

Not "what does it cost to live in Dubai monthly" but "how much cash do I need available in the first six weeks, before any local income arrives." That's a fundamentally different — and usually larger — number, and it's the one that actually determines whether a relocation is financially comfortable or a scramble.
