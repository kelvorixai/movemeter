Shipping your household goods to the UAE: cost breakdown by method

Sea freight, air freight, and excess baggage sit on a clear cost-versus-speed curve — the right method depends on how much you're moving and how soon you need it.

Three main methods cover most household relocations to the UAE, each with a distinct cost structure: sea freight (cheapest per volume, slowest), air freight (fast, priced by weight, expensive at volume), and excess baggage or a small courier shipment (fine for a handful of boxes, impractical for furniture). Choosing the wrong method for the volume being moved is the single biggest cost mistake in this part of a relocation.

Sea freight: cheapest for full households

Priced by volume (typically per cubic meter or per shipping container — a 20ft container suits roughly a 1-2 bedroom household, a 40ft a larger one). A full 1-bedroom household commonly runs somewhere in the AED 6,000-12,000 range door-to-door depending on origin country and consolidation versus a dedicated container; transit time is typically 4-10 weeks depending on origin, which is the main tradeoff against the lower cost.

Air freight: fast but priced by weight

Priced by chargeable weight (the greater of actual or volumetric weight), air freight typically costs several times more than sea freight per kilo moved but arrives in days rather than weeks. It suits smaller, time-sensitive shipments — work equipment, essentials for the first few weeks — rather than full household contents, where the weight-based pricing becomes expensive quickly.

Excess baggage and small courier shipments

For anything that fits in a handful of extra suitcases, airline excess baggage fees or a courier service are usually cheaper and simpler than formal freight, but pricing is per-kilo and climbs fast — this method suits personal effects, not furniture or bulk household goods.

A "door-to-door" quote that excludes customs clearance or destination handling can understate the real cost significantly — confirming exactly what's bundled is the single most useful question to ask a freight provider before booking.

The actual question before booking

Not "which shipping method is cheapest" but "what's my actual volume and how time-sensitive is it" — sea freight for full households with 6+ weeks of lead time, air freight for smaller time-sensitive shipments, and baggage allowance for the rest. Getting two or three door-to-door quotes for the same volume is the most reliable way to sanity-check any single provider's price.
